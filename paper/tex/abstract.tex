A diffusion-personalisation comparison was recorded as unpaired evidence for
a mechanism claim. Re-reading the artifacts shows that the pairing was
present: all three arms used the same \NPairs{} prompts and matched training
budgets, while the registered endpoint reduced each arm to one dispersion
number and thereby erased the pairs.

At that endpoint, the recorded dispersion ratio is compatible with
\DispLower{}--\DispUpper{} at 95\% confidence, so it supports no separation.
When the pairing is recovered at the level of the recorded generations, the
composed arm is lower on identity fidelity for \IdentityDown{} of
\NPairs{} pairs (sign-test \(p=\IdentitySignP{}\)) and on prompt fidelity for
\PromptDown{} of \NPairs{} pairs (\(p=\PromptSignP{}\)). With
\NPairs{} pairs, the smallest two-sided exact sign-test probability is
\FloorAtFour{}, so the registered endpoint could not reach \Alpha{} whatever
the four outcomes. An unregistered paired \(t\) test crosses that threshold
only by changing the endpoint and assuming a distributional shape that four
points cannot establish.

We therefore report no separation in this small comparison at its registered
endpoint and pre-register the experiment that could settle it:
\DesignPerConcept{} pairs per concept across \DesignConcepts{} concepts, with
\DesignTolerance{} tolerated reversals and a target of \Alpha{}. The sized
protocol has not been run and remains unauthorised.
