The registered paired protocol for this diffusion-personalisation comparison
found no identity separation. Across \RunPairs{} identity pairs the composed
arm was lower on \RunIdentityLower{} and higher on \RunIdentityHigher{}
(exact sign-test \(p=\RunIdentitySignP{}\)). The mean paired-difference
interval \RunIdentityCiLo{} to \RunIdentityCiHi{} includes zero, so the
confirmatory test does not separate the arms.

That design was sized from a historical reanalysis. A recorded unpaired
comparison had collapsed the same \NPairs{} matched prompts into one
dispersion per arm. Recovered as pairs, the composed arm was lower on
identity fidelity for \IdentityDown{} of \NPairs{} prompts (sign-test
\(p=\IdentitySignP{}\)), a test whose attainable floor at that size is
\FloorAtFour{}. The registered design therefore used \DesignTotal{} pairs
across \DesignConcepts{} concepts.

Prompt fidelity, the registered secondary endpoint, was lower for the
composed arm on \RunPromptLower{} of \RunPairs{} pairs
(\(p=\RunPromptSignP{}\)) and is not promoted. Dispersion ratios reversed
relative to the historical \SDRatioComposed{} (\RunDispersionDog{} and
\RunDispersionClock{}) and are descriptive only. The protocol was declared
before training (evidence ID E-PREDECL) and realised every pair
with \RunFailures{} failures. The scoped claim is that a pre-declared paired
sign test at this size does not separate the arms on identity fidelity.
