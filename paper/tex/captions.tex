\newcommand{\CapCapacity}{%
Two ways of spending the same parameters. A subject-driven personalisation run
teaches a concept to a latent diffusion backbone through low-rank adapters on
the attention projections. The upper bar trains one adapter across both
attention types at once. The lower bar trains two adapters separately, one on
the cross-attention projections and one on the self-attention projections, and
switches both on at generation time. The two adapters together train
\CrossParams{} and \SelfParams{} parameters, which sum to the \JointParams{}
that the single adapter trains; the split is a partition of the same parameter
set rather than a change of capacity. Both arms were trained at
\TrainSteps{} steps, learning rate \LearningRate{}, adapter rank \LoraRank{},
resolution \Resolution{} and seed \TrainSeed{}. Counts are read from the
training records written by the runs.%
}

\newcommand{\CapCollapse}{%
The values a dispersion endpoint throws away. Each line follows one background
prompt across three arms of the same personalisation study: a single adapter
trained across both attention types, two separately trained adapters composed
at generation time, and the same composition with the self-attention adapter
switched off. All three arms were generated over the same \NPairs{} prompts, so
each line is a like-for-like sequence. The number printed beneath each arm is
the endpoint the recorded comparison used: the standard deviation of that arm's
\NPairs{} identity fidelities, computed across prompts. Collapsing four values
into one leaves a single number per arm, and a comparison of two such numbers
has nothing left to pair. The legend uses P1--P4, stable row codes in the
recorded order, rather than printing the background-prompt text.%
}

\newcommand{\CapDispersion}{%
The recorded comparison, carried through to the interval it supports. Each row
is a ratio of dispersions: the standard deviation of identity fidelity across
\NPairs{} background prompts for one arm, divided by the same quantity for the
arm trained as a single adapter. Points are the observed ratios and bars are
95\% confidence intervals from an $F$ distribution at \NPairs{} generations per
arm, which is the interval an unpaired comparison of two dispersions at this
size admits. The dashed line marks equal dispersion. Both intervals contain it.
The interval assumes the two arms are independent; they are not, because they
share the background prompts, and that assumption is the one the unpaired
comparison already makes. The horizontal axis is logarithmic.%
}

\newcommand{\CapPaired}{%
The same generations, analysed as the pairs they were run as. Each point is one
background prompt, and its position is the composed arm's value minus the value
of the arm trained as a single adapter at that same prompt, for the endpoint
named above the panel. Identity fidelity is the quantity the recorded
comparison aggregated into a dispersion; prompt fidelity is recorded in the
same files and was not used. The row labelled \emph{mean} carries the 95\%
confidence interval for the mean paired difference as a grey band, with the
mean as the vertical rule inside it, and the note beneath it gives that
interval together with the exact two-sided sign-test probability on the same
pairs. With \NPairs{} pairs the smallest value that probability can take is
\FloorAtFour{}, so the sign test in either panel stays above a conventional
threshold whatever the points do. The dashed vertical rule is zero paired
change; marker fill and shape redundantly encode whether the composed arm is
lower or higher. The pair rows use the same P1--P4 row codes as the paired
table; the codes are not prompt content.%
}

\newcommand{\CapEndpointMap}{%
A descriptive paired map of two recorded endpoints. For each of the \NPairs{}
background-prompt rows, the horizontal coordinate is prompt fidelity for the
composed arm minus the jointly trained arm and the vertical coordinate is the
corresponding identity-fidelity difference. Both axes are in the same
fidelity-score units and share one scale, so a change of a given size has the
same length in either direction. The arrows start at zero only to make each
within-prompt change vector visible, and each stops just short of its marker;
they are not trajectories and carry no causal interpretation. P1--P4 are
stable row codes in the recorded order and intentionally do not reproduce
prompt text. Triangles are the composed arm and squares are the
cross-attention-only arm. Prompt fidelity was recorded but was not the
registered historical endpoint, so this four-row diagnostic supplies no pooled
effect, correlation, or general-population inference. All \PromptDown{}
composed-arm prompt-fidelity changes are negative, while the identity changes
do not share one sign; the figure is a separation of coordinates, not evidence
of a trade-off.%
}

\newcommand{\CapFloor}{%
What a sign test can reach, before any data. Each curve gives the smallest
two-sided probability an exact sign test can return at a given number of pairs:
the lowest curve when every pair falls the same way, the others when one or two
pairs fall the other way. The quantity depends on the number of pairs alone, so
it is fixed before an experiment is run and no effect size changes it. The
dashed line is \Alpha{}. Four sizes are marked and explained in the key:
(a) the comparison re-analysed here, with \NPairs{} pairs, sits above the line
at every outcome; (b) \MinNAny{} pairs is the first size that can cross it, and
then only on unanimity; (c) the pre-registered design's \DesignPerConcept{}
pairs per concept reach \Alpha{} while \DesignTolerance{} pairs disagree; and
(d) the registered run pools \DesignTotal{} pairs across its two concept
strata, reaching \Alpha{} while allowing \DesignPooledTolerance{} pairs to
disagree, and its all-agreeing floor is \SignFloorRealised{}. Mark (a)
identifies the recorded \(n=\NPairs{}\) case, so the floor is a design limit
rather than an observed effect.%
}

\newcommand{\CapArms}{%
The three adapters on disk, as their training records describe them. Trainable
parameter counts, wall clock and final training loss are read from the manifest
each run wrote. The two adapters in the lower block are the ones composed at
generation time; their parameter counts sum to the count of the adapter trained
across both attention types, which is what makes the two arms matched on
capacity. All three were trained at the same budget, learning rate, rank,
resolution and seed.%
}

\newcommand{\CapPairs}{%
Per-prompt values behind the recorded comparison, and the differences the
recorded endpoint discarded. Rows are stable P1--P4 codes in the recorded
background-prompt order; the analysis joins arms on the private prompt key but
does not print that text here. \emph{Joint} is the arm trained as one adapter across
both attention types; \emph{composed} is the arm whose two adapters were
trained separately and switched on together. Differences are composed minus
joint, so a negative value means the composed arm scored lower at that prompt.
The final row is the endpoint the recorded comparison used: the standard
deviation of each arm's four identity fidelities across prompts, which is one
number per arm and therefore admits no paired difference.%
}

\newcommand{\CapProtocol}{%
The paired experiment, locked before the run and then executed once. The pairing unit, the arms, the
endpoints and the tests were fixed so that no choice among them could be
made after the results were seen. The number of background prompts is the
recorded grid, unchanged. The number of generation seeds is the smallest that
leaves each concept able to reach \Alpha{} on an exact sign test with at least
one pair falling the wrong way; every other size in the table follows from it.
The training budget is that of the arms whose comparison this protocol
re-tests. The table is that locked declaration.%
}

\newcommand{\CapProtocolFlow}{%
The pre-registered sequence, shown as the specification that was then executed once. A pair is
one concept, one background prompt and one generation seed; the jointly trained
and composed arms are matched on the recorded training budget and controls. The
primary endpoint is the within-pair change in identity fidelity, tested by one
pooled exact two-sided sign test at \Alpha{}; concept-specific estimates are
descriptive strata, and the registered secondary check sits beside them. The
two strips beneath the boxes list the controls every arm shares and the
derived design counts: \DesignConcepts{} concepts, \DesignPerConcept{} pairs
per concept and \DesignTotal{} pairs in total, yielding \DesignGenerations{}
generations. The analysis lock requires joining by the pair key, fixing the
protocol before generation, unblinding once and admitting no re-run. The
banner reports the declaration time and the realised pair count from the
bound run; the boxes themselves remain the specification, not the primary
result.%
}

\newcommand{\CapPairedRun}{%
The registered paired run on all \RunPairs{} realised pairs. Panel A is the
registered primary endpoint, identity fidelity; Panel B is the declared
secondary endpoint, prompt fidelity, drawn narrower and on a tinted ground to
mark its rank outside the confirmatory family. In each panel a point is one
concept--prompt--seed pair, placed at the composed arm's value minus the
jointly trained arm's; within a concept the three rows are the three
generation seeds, in seed order from the top. The row labelled \emph{pooled
mean} carries the 95\% interval for the mean paired difference across all
\RunPairs{} pairs as a grey band, with the mean as the vertical rule inside
it. The note in each panel gives the number of pairs on which the composed
arm was lower and the two-sided exact sign-test probability on those pairs:
Panel A, \RunIdentityLower{} of \RunPairs{}, $p=\RunIdentitySignP{}$, with the
mean interval from \RunIdentityCiLo{} to \RunIdentityCiHi{}; Panel B,
\RunPromptLower{} of \RunPairs{}, $p=\RunPromptSignP{}$, reported at that
secondary rank. The dashed vertical rule is zero paired change; marker fill
and shape encode whether the composed arm is lower or higher.%
}

\newcommand{\CapConceptSlopes}{%
Paired identity and prompt-fidelity slopes in the registered run. Each segment
joins the jointly trained arm (open marker) to the composed arm (filled
marker) for one matched concept--prompt--seed pair, and is coloured by the
sign of that paired change, composed lower or composed higher. The column
strips name the two concepts, dog and clock, with their pair counts; identity
fidelity is the registered primary endpoint and occupies the upper row of
facets, prompt fidelity the declared secondary endpoint below it. The figure
contains all \RunPairs{} realised pairs (\RunPairsPerConcept{} per concept),
with \RunSeedCount{} generation seeds across \RunPromptCount{} prompts. Slopes
are descriptive paired observations: they add no second confirmatory test,
estimate no population beyond these two concepts, and leave the identity claim
to the primary endpoint. The plotted rows are derived solely from the bound
paired-run analysis record; the declaration remains bound under digest
\PredeclarationShaShort{}.%
}
